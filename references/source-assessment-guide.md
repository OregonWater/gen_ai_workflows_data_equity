Use one editable Markdown assessment per source in `references/source-assessments/`, named with its registry key. These are project reading records, not raw transcripts or additional session summaries. They are public: confidential excerpts, restricted locations and candid private notes belong in ignored `nonpublic_notes/`.

## Assessment format

1. **Reading scope:** source key, citation, version, link, date, reader and exact portions checked. Distinguish assistant screening from the author's reading.
2. **Author's central argument:** a faithful paraphrase, labeled with who prepared it. Do not substitute the reader's preferred claim.
3. **Key terms and passages:** page/section locator, source wording or a labeled paraphrase, and the distinction that needs checking. Use short quotations sparingly; do not copy the paper into the assessment.
4. **Support:** theoretical argument, formal analysis, examples, empirical methods or synthesis. Assess whether that support warrants the claim; mark unreviewed methods and results explicitly.
5. **Limits:** scope, objections and what the source does not establish for this project.
6. **Author's reading response:** agreement, objection, questions, connections to TAPs and choices about terminology. Leave this pending until the author supplies it. Assistant suggestions must be separately attributed.
7. **Possible use:** context, definition, counterargument or candidate talk claim, with verification still needed. Register substantive claims in knowledge-base/04 before slide or speaker-note use.

Record conceptual changes and decisions in the dated session. Reconcile current synthesis into knowledge-base/03 and relevant pages. The source assessment contains detailed reading analysis; it does not replace session provenance or the claims register. Update `references/sources.yml` when the actual reading scope advances. Do not count a populated form as a completed assessment.
