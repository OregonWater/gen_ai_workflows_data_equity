## 1. Reading scope

Don Ihde, 1990, “Program One: A Phenomenology of Technics,” chapter 5 of *Technology and the Lifeworld: From Garden to Earth*, Indiana University Press, pp. 72–123. Registry key: `ihde-1990-phenomenology-technics`; bibliography key: `ihde1990technics`.

## 2. Author's central argument

**Assistant paraphrase of the selected passages:** Ihde investigates structures of human–technology relations through examples of bodily engagement and interpretation. Technologies can become incorporated into perception or provide something people read to understand a referent. Such mediation transforms experience rather than merely adding reach to an otherwise unchanged experience. This is a reading of the selected passages, not a summary of the complete chapter.

## 4. Support and strength of argument

Phenomenological argument developed through examples and structural distinctions. These passages provide direct textual grounds for attributing mediation and transformative non-neutrality to Ihde. They do not provide an experiment, effect size, statistical distribution, or empirical test of TAPs.

Candidate mapping: ME-01 (mediation), ME-02 (transformation) and ME-03 (design) in the claims register. The source's argument and the project's extension remain separate.

## 5. Limits and objections

- The selected passages do not establish a universal thesis that every kind of access to the world is technologically mediated. Ihde explicitly distinguishes experience with and without an artifact.
- The design examples support an influence on experience, not complete determination by a designer.
- Amplification/reduction does not itself identify whose knowledge is socially privileged, establish injustice, or select an equity paradigm.
- Nuclear-plant history and other cited historical examples were encountered as Ihde's illustrations; their underlying records were not independently checked. No slide claim about those events is proposed.
- The author's onions, separate envelopes and shading are an explanatory proposal, not a diagram or quantitative model supplied by Ihde.
