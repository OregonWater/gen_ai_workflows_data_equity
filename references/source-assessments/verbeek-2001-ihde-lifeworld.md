## 2. Author's central argument

**Assistant synthesis of the selected pages:** Verbeek explains Ihde's technological mediation through bodily and interpretive perception, embodiment and hermeneutic relations, and amplification/reduction. He also argues against understanding mediation as a bridge between an unchanged, pre-existing subject and object: the relation helps constitute both. This is Verbeek's interpretation and elaboration of Ihde, not an independent empirical corroboration.

## 5. Limits and objections

- The selected account supports an influence of design on mediated experience; it does not isolate design as the sole driver.
- “Predetermines” in the p. 128 discussion concerns the aspect and manner offered by a representation. The project's plain-language claim should avoid converting it into universal determination of human understanding.
- Neither perceptual amplification/reduction nor the co-constitution argument validates the author's shaded distributions of privileged possibilities.
- Claims about access inequalities, institutional selection, injustice and equity need their own argument and supporting passages. They do not follow solely from non-neutrality.
- The two arrows and envelopes are a project simplification. Their mathematical independence or probabilistic meaning is not established by this source.
