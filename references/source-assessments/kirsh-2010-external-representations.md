## 2. Author's central argument

**Assistant-prepared synthesis for author review:** Kirsh argues that working with external representations can change the possibilities of thought as well as its costs. This is a claim about interactive activity, not only retaining information. Sections 3–9 develop proposed mechanisms through examples and argument; the conclusion returns to manipulation of representations. This paraphrase does not establish the mechanisms or their effectiveness in TAPs.

**Author's own summary:** supplied in section 6. It identifies enhanced cognitive power as Kirsh's overarching claim and treats the paper as a characterization of externalization's functions, with questions about extending its interaction framework to GenAI. Section 6 is preserved as the author's reading response, not silently reconciled with the assistant's comments below.

The author identifies a broad cognitive-enhancement claim; this assessment keeps that ambition distinct from the particular support checked here.

## 5. Limits and objections

- This source does not test the TAPs workflow or contemporary conversational GenAI.
- Applying its concepts to this project is an interpretive step that needs explanation.
- Durable records are selective artifacts; their presence does not establish accurate reasoning, faithful process recovery or improved public understanding.
- **Author's objections and questions:** supplied in section 6; remain open. They concern interaction, closed/open worlds, the cost-only explanation and the specificity of externalization as a concept.

### Assistant response to the first-pass notes

1. **Separate model change from environmental change.** If model parameters are assumed to stay fixed, that does not establish that no external change occurred: the prompt, displayed response, edited file or repository state can change. Kirsh's definition does not require the external tool itself to learn. This is a proposed application of section 1, not a claim about a particular model's architecture or agency.
2. **Treat closed/open as a task-level analytical question.** Kirsh's setup removes access to new information sources and runnable processes. Whether a chatbot interaction fits that setup depends on the task, inputs and available operations; a text interface alone does not decide it. Distinguish new information, reformulation and factual verification before classifying the exchange.
3. **Qualify the statement about definition.** The paper is not a dedicated taxonomy or operational definition of externalization. Section 3 nonetheless offers a more explicit account through materializing structure and enabling shared reference. The project's jointly produced representations may require extending that account rather than treating externalization as undefined.
4. **Separate functions from drivers and guaranteed outcomes.** Your notes identify a rich set of possibilities. The paper's proposed functions do not by themselves explain why a particular person externalized something or prove a benefit in each case. Note also that re-representation is more specific than representation: the change of form is part of the issue.
5. **Distinguish persistence from independence of interpretation.** Sections 4–5 can sharpen the AI-assisted/AI-dependent connection: retaining a file supports a different question from retaining enough prior states and context to examine a transformation. Persistence also permits interpretations the creator did not anticipate. Your TAPs connection is a design interpretation to assess, not a result established by Kirsh.
6. **Test the construction analogy.** Section 9 deserves a critical second pass. What a constrained physical or formal construction establishes cannot automatically be transferred to the existence of a polished GenAI narrative. This is potentially useful for the talk's resistant-world framing, provided the distinction is made explicit.

## 6. Author's reading response

To be supplied by Francisco J. Guerrero; assistant suggestions are not his assessment.

- **What I take the paper to argue:**
The author's full claim is that external representations enhance cognitive power and allow people to think more powerfully.
- **Where I agree, disagree or remain uncertain:**
I find their definition about interaction interesting and potentially useful even if its to argue against: "By 'interactive' I mean a back and forth process: a person alters the outside world, the changed world alters the person, and the dynamics continue." I wonder if this definition of interaction can be extended to exchanges with GenAI; we do not alter an outside (or internal) world, the structure of the representational space for AI does not change as part of the exchange, the human representational space can change though. They also defined a closed world as "consisting of a person and an external representation-a diagram, illustration, spoken instruction, or written problem statement" and immediately ask "why do people do more than just think in their heads?" In a way they seem to define externalization as something inherent to interacting with both the open or closed world. Then the question becomes, does a chatbot provides us with access to an open or closed world?
Then, they introduce an interesting analogy between the "human 'cognitive operating system'" and "a computer system that has memory systems and scratch pads in different media and locations" to elaborate on the concept of distributed or extened cognition while criticizing the "conceptual monopoly" of performance costs. He then argues that additional drivers can modulate the externalization process by changing the cost structure of the inferential landscape, the provision of a structure that can serve as a shareable object of thought, the creation of persistent referents, the facilitation of representation, the facilitation of the computation of more explicit encoding of information, the construction of arbitrarily complex structures, and supporting thought coordination.
In short, the paper does provide a richer description of the potential drivers or outcomes of externalization without presenting an exact definition.
- **Connection to a specific TAPs artifact or episode:**
I see a connection to the core design decision for TAPs about being AI-Assisted and not AI-Dependent implemented through the creation of a durable record (the repository)
- **Implication for Hybrid Reasoning Externalization:**
It could be used to provide a characterization of what do we mean by externalization. On the Hybrid Reasoning portion, it formulates questions about what accounts for interaction and the types of interactions we sustain with the open and closed world.
- **Terminology I would adopt, qualify or reject, and why:**
I would definitely consider this reference for a description of how externalization looks like in our daily lives.
